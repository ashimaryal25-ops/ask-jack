# Janome MC550E & MC9850 — Needles & Maintenance
machine: Janome MC550E / MC9850
category: embroidery — maintenance

## Overview
Keeping fresh, sharp needles on the machine and regularly brushing away lint buildup from the hook race ensures crisp stitches, prevents thread breakage, and protects the machine's internal motors.

---

## 1. Replacing Needles & Needle Selection Chart

A dull, bent, or incorrect needle causes over 80% of embroidery defects (including skipped stitches, frayed thread, and fabric snagging). Replace your needle after every 8–10 hours of active stitching, or immediately if you hear a popping sound while penetrating fabric.

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-replacing-needle.png | Replacing Needles and Needle Selection Chart]

### Needle Selection Chart for Embroidery:
- **Janome / Organ Blue Tip Needle (Size #11/75)**:
  - **Best for**: Standard woven fabrics, fine knits, satin, silk, and general embroidery.
  - **Feature**: Specialized pierce point designed to prevent skipped stitches on high-density embroidery.
- **Janome / Organ Purple Tip Needle (Size #14/90)**:
  - **Best for**: Stretchy knits, fleece, velour, sweaters, synthetic materials.
  - **Feature**: Ballpoint cobra head prevents fabric threads from cutting and eliminates puckering on knits.
- **Janome / Organ Red Tip Needle (Size #14/90)**:
  - **Best for**: Heavy fabrics, canvas, denim, thick twill, multi-layer tote bags.
  - **Feature**: Heavy-duty shaft resists needle deflection on thick woven fabrics.

### How to Replace the Needle:
1. Turn off the machine power switch or press the lockout key.
2. Turn the handwheel toward you to raise the needle to its highest position.
3. Lower the presser foot.
4. Loosen the needle clamp screw by turning it counterclockwise with the screwdriver key.
5. Pull the old needle straight down and discard it.
6. Insert the new needle with the **FLAT SIDE FACING TO THE REAR (away from you)**.
7. Push the needle straight up into the clamp as far as it will go until it touches the stopper pin.
8. Tighten the needle clamp screw firmly with the screwdriver key.

## 2. Cleaning the Hook Race & Removing Lint

Lint and thread scraps naturally accumulate inside the bobbin race during high-speed embroidery. If neglected, lint will pack under the bobbin holder, altering bobbin tension and causing thread jams.

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-cleaning-hook-area.png | Cleaning Hook Race and Bobbin Area]

### Cleaning Procedure:
1. Turn off the power switch and unplug the machine.
2. Remove the needle and presser foot.
3. Remove the two needle plate screws using the screwdriver key and lift off the needle plate.
4. Take out the high-tension bobbin holder (yellow dot).
5. Use the lint brush to thoroughly sweep away all lint and dust from the hook race, thread trimmer blade, and feed dogs.
6. Wipe the inside of the hook race with a soft, dry lint-free cloth. (Never use canned air or blow into the machine, as moisture and lint will be forced deeper into the bearings).
7. Reinsert the bobbin holder, aligning the knob with the machine stopper.
8. Reattach the needle plate, presser foot, and needle.

## 3. General Care & Safety Rules
- **Power Off When Adjusting**: Always power off or lock out the machine before swapping needles or changing the bobbin holder.
- **Keep Foreign Objects Clear**: Never place pins, scissors, or rulers within the movement path of the embroidery carriage arm.
- **Oiling**: The machine requires specialized mechanical oil on internal shafts periodically; this is handled exclusively by ICL staff (Josh & Eric). Do not apply generic WD-40 or household oil.
