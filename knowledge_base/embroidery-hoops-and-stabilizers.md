# Embroidery Hoops & Stabilizers Guide
machine: Janome MC550E / MC9850
category: embroidery — hardware

## Overview
Proper stabilization and hooping are the two most critical factors determining the quality of your embroidery. If fabric shifts or stretches inside the hoop during stitching, the design will pucker, outline stitches will misalign, and the fabric can bunch up under the needle.

---

## Choosing the Right Stabilizer

Match your stabilizer to your fabric type:

| Stabilizer Type | Best For | How It Works & Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cut-Away** | Stretchy or knit fabrics: t-shirts, hoodies, sweatshirts, polo shirts, beanies, athletic wear | **Offers the highest support**. Prevents stretchy fabrics from distorting during high-density stitching. Stays permanently on the back of the garment — trim away excess around the design when finished. |
| **2. Tear-Away** | Stable woven fabrics that do not stretch: denim, canvas, twill, leather, vinyl, non-stretchy tote bags | Provides temporary stability during stitching, then easily tears cleanly away from the seams after you unhoop. |
| **3. Water-Soluble Sticky Back** | Hard-to-hoop items: cuffs, collars, socks, small pockets, or delicate fabrics | Sticky adhesive holds the fabric firmly to the stabilizer without clamping marks. **Usage tip:** Make sure the shiny side faces up when sandwiching in the hoop; use a seam ripper to score around the inner edge and peel off the release paper to expose the adhesive. Dissolves in water. |
| **4. Water-Soluble Topping** | Textured or high-pile fabrics: terry cloth towels, fleece, velour, corduroy, sweaters | Place on **TOP** of the fabric before sewing. It holds pile/loops down so stitches sit crisp on the surface instead of sinking into the fibers. Dissolves instantly with a damp sponge or wash. |

## ICL Hoop Sizes & Machine Compatibility

Always choose the smallest hoop that comfortably fits your design to ensure maximum fabric tension and reduce puckering.

### Janome MC9850 Hoops:
- **FA10a**: 1.6" × 3.9" (40mm × 100mm) — for cuffs, sleeves, socks, pocket edges
- **SQ14a**: 5.5" × 5.5" (140mm × 140mm) — for standard chest logos, patches, small designs
- **RE20a**: 6.7" × 7.9" (170mm × 200mm) — for larger graphics, tote bags, front shirts

### Janome MC550E Hoops:
- **SQ14b**: 5.5" × 5.5" (140mm × 140mm) — standard square hoop for left-chest logos & patches
- **RE20b**: 7.9" × 5.5" (200mm × 140mm) — rectangular wide designs
- **SQ20b**: 7.9" × 7.9" (200mm × 200mm) — large square hoop for hoodie graphics, tote bags
- **RE36b**: 7.9" × 14.2" (200mm × 360mm) — extra-long hoop for jacket backs, banners, sleeve runs

## How to Set Fabric in the Embroidery Hoop

1. **Loosen the hoop clamp screw** on the outer hoop and separate the inner hoop from the outer hoop.
2. **Cut stabilizer**: Cut a piece of stabilizer slightly larger than the outer dimensions of your selected hoop.
3. **Layer the sandwich**:
   - Lay the outer hoop on a flat, solid table.
   - Lay the stabilizer over the outer hoop.
   - Lay your fabric right-side up on top of the stabilizer.
4. **Align with template**: Place the transparent plastic template into the inner hoop to align the center point with your target location on the fabric.
5. **Press inner hoop down**: Push the inner hoop down into the outer hoop, sandwiching the fabric and stabilizer together firmly.
6. **Tighten and test**:
   - Tighten the outer screw securely by hand.
   - Gently pull the fabric edges around the perimeter until smooth.
   - Tap the fabric: it should feel taut and smooth like a drum skin, with no wrinkles or distortion of the fabric grain.
   - Remove the plastic template before attaching the hoop to the machine!
