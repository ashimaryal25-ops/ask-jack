# Janome MC550E — Screen Operations & Machine Sewing
machine: Janome MC550E
category: embroidery — operation

## Overview
The Janome MC550E features a full-color visual touch screen and intuitive physical operating buttons that guide the embroidery process from file selection to final trimming.

---

## 1. Machine Operating Buttons

There are two primary physical buttons located directly above the needle area on the front of the machine head:

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-machine-operating-buttons.png | Janome MC550E Operating Buttons — Start/Stop and Thread Cutter Buttons]

### ① Start/Stop Button
- **Green light**: The machine is ready to sew. Press to start embroidery.
- **Red light**: The machine is currently running. Press to pause or stop embroidery at any time.
- **Flashing or no light**: Machine is locked or an error condition exists (such as raised presser foot).

### ② Thread Cutter Button (Scissor Icon)
- Located directly below the Start/Stop button on the front machine head.
- Press this physical button when you finish sewing to automatically trim both the upper thread and the bobbin thread. The needle bar will automatically rise to its highest position after trimming.

### ⚠️ IMPORTANT: "Lower the Presser Foot" Warning
If you press the Start/Stop button while the presser foot is in the raised position, the machine will not stitch and a yellow warning box displaying **"Lower the Presser Foot" (③)** will pop up on the touch screen.
**Fix**: Simply lower the black presser foot lever located behind the needle assembly, then press the Start/Stop button again.

## 2. Attaching the Hoop to the Carriage Arm

1. Raise the presser foot to its highest position.
2. Turn the handwheel toward you to ensure the needle is raised all the way up.
3. Pass the hooped fabric under the needle and presser foot from the front-left.
4. Align the mounting pins on the hoop bracket with the receiving slots on the carriage arm.
5. Push the clamp lever down firmly until it clicks into the locked position.

## 3. Ready to Sew Screen & Function Keys

When you select an embroidery design from your USB drive, the **Ready to Sew** window displays key details about the job:

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-ready-to-sew.png | Ready to Sew Screen Information]

- **Design Preview**: Visual depiction of the current design and position inside the hoop.
- **Hoop Indicator**: Displays the required hoop size (e.g. SQ14b, RE20b).
- **Stitch Count & Estimated Time**: Shows remaining stitch count and approximate sewing duration.
- **Color Sequence Bar**: Shows each thread color in order of sewing, with the active color highlighted.
- **Jog Keys (Arrow buttons)**: Use to fine-tune the starting position of the embroidery within the hoop.
- **Stitch Back / Forward Keys**: Use if the thread breaks to rewind 10–30 stitches before restarting so no gaps appear in your design.

## 4. Checking Boundaries with the Trace Key (CRITICAL SAFETY STEP)

**ALWAYS run a boundary trace before pressing Start!** This ensures the embroidery foot and needle will not strike the hard plastic edge of the hoop frame during sewing.

1. In the Ready to Sew window, tap the **Trace Key** (the hoop icon with dashed arrows).
2. The machine carriage will automatically move the hoop along the outermost perimeter boundary of your design.
3. Watch the needle position closely as the hoop moves:
   - Ensure the embroidery foot has at least a 10mm (0.4 inch) clearance from all four sides of the hoop frame.
   - If the foot comes dangerously close to the plastic frame, tap Cancel, select a larger hoop size or resize your artwork in Artistic Digitizer.

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-trace-function.png | Checking Boundaries with the Trace Key]

## 5. Starting to Sew & Trimming Thread Tails

1. Ensure the hooped fabric lies flat on the machine bed without catching on the machine arm or wide table.
2. Lower the presser foot.
3. Hold the upper thread tail lightly with your left hand.
4. Press the **Start/Stop button** (button turns green).
5. Let the machine sew **5 to 6 stitches**, then press the Start/Stop button again to pause.
6. Using sharp embroidery snips, cut the excess starting thread tail right down close to the fabric surface. (This prevents the loose tail from getting stitched over and creating an ugly knot).
7. Press the Start/Stop button again to resume high-speed automatic embroidery.

[IMAGE: https://xyaewwhcelutcoosbawk.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images/embroidery/diagram-starting-to-sew.png | Starting to Sew and Trimming Thread Tails]

## 6. Changing Thread Colors During Multi-Color Designs

1. When a color section completes, the machine automatically trims the thread, raises the needle, and beeps.
2. Cut the current upper thread near the spool pin, and pull the remaining thread out through the needle from below. (Never pull thread backwards up through the machine).
3. Place the new color spool on the spool pin and rethread through guides 1 to 7.
4. Lower the presser foot, hold the thread tail, press Start, sew 5–6 stitches, pause, trim the tail, and resume!
